# Dictate with Mach

Mach turns speech into text in the Mac app where you are already working. This
guide covers shortcuts, output modes, languages, personalization, and the habits
that produce the best result.

## Choose a shortcut

### Push to talk

1. Place the cursor in an editable text field.
2. Hold `Fn` and wait for the listening indicator.
3. Speak naturally.
4. Release `Fn` to finish and insert the text.

### Hands free

Press `Fn + Space` once to start and again to finish. You can also double-tap
the push-to-talk shortcut to keep that session open hands-free. Press `Esc` at
any time to cancel without inserting text.

Open **Mach → Dictation Settings → Shortcuts** to change either shortcut. Mach
also provides defaults that work well with external keyboards.

## Choose an output mode

### Refined

Refined mode removes filler, repairs punctuation and grammar, adds useful
formatting, and applies your tone, dictionary, language, and writing-system
preferences. It aims to preserve the meaning you supplied.

Use Refined for email, messages, notes, briefs, prompts, and other writing that
should be ready to use.

### Verbatim

Verbatim mode, labeled **Pure STT (verbatim)** in some app versions, returns the
speech-to-text result without AI cleanup. Use it when exact wording matters
more than presentation.

## Choose a language

Select the exact language or regional variant when you plan to speak one
language. Mach offers 87 language and regional options.

Choose Multilingual mode when your speech naturally moves between English,
Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Russian, Portuguese, Japanese, Italian, and
Dutch. Each phrase stays in the language you spoke.

## Choose a writing system

When **Writing style** appears for the selected language, Refined mode can keep
the original script or use Latin letters. Hindi can use Hinglish, Japanese can
use Romaji, and other supported scripts provide their own Latin-output option.

Romanization changes the writing system only. It does not translate the text or
merge multiple spoken languages.

## Set the tone

- **Auto** adapts to the current app.
- **Casual** works well for informal chat.
- **Professional** suits business communication.
- **Neutral** keeps the writing direct and general-purpose.

Tone is applied during Refined cleanup and does not affect Verbatim output.

## Add dictionary terms

Use the custom dictionary for names, products, abbreviations, and specialist
terms that require an exact spelling.

1. Open **Mach → Dictation Settings → Dictionary**.
2. Enter the term exactly as it should appear.
3. Choose **Add**.
4. Test it in a short Refined dictation.

Keep the dictionary focused. Add one preferred spelling, remove outdated or
conflicting variants, and do not use phonetic approximations. Dictionary terms
guide final cleanup; they do not retrain speech recognition.

## Improve dictation results

- Choose a focused language unless you are actually code-switching.
- Select the microphone closest to you.
- Reduce background audio where practical.
- Speak in complete phrases at a natural pace.
- Add repeatedly misspelled specialist terms to the dictionary.
- Use Refined for formatting and Verbatim for a close transcript.

If recognition is consistently wrong, test a short phrase in TextEdit. This
helps separate microphone or language problems from limitations in a particular
destination app.
